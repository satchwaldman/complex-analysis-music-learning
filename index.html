The one-paragraph story

For real part σ > 1, the identity |ζ(σ+it)|² is literally a Tenney-weighted sum of cosines measuring how well the “tuning” selected by t approximates every rational interval. Setting t = 2πN/ln 2 makes that tuning the N-tone equal temperament. Continued to the critical line σ = ½, the peaks of the Riemann–Siegel Z(t) land right on the historically favored EDOs — 12, 19, 31, 41, 53. That last step is analytic continuation, not a theorem about tuning, and telling the two apart is the whole point of this site.

What is actually known — and what isn't

Rigorous

For σ > 1, |ζ|² is a weighted error metric over rationals — an algebraic identity. Regular temperament theory (vals, monzos, TE norms) is solid linear algebra over free abelian groups.

Heuristic

Extending the tuning interpretation to σ = ½ assumes the property survives analytic continuation. The wiki itself calls this an open question — “empirically it seems to.”

Numerical

The match between |Z| peaks and the good EDOs is an excellent empirical fit, verified by direct computation — but there is no theorem N good ⟺ |Z| peaks near 2πN/ln2.

The first peer-reviewed treatment is Alexandre Guillet, “Elementary spectrum for the dissonance curve: from biophysics to number theory of musical harmony,” Journal of Mathematics and Music (2026), DOI 10.1080/17459737.2026.2628778. Before it, the correspondence lived entirely on mailing lists and the Xenharmonic Wiki. Two questions it leaves open drive the research angle: why the 1/√n weighting? and is composite “double-counting” a bug or a timbre model?
