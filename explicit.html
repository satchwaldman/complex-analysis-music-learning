The explicit formula — primes out of zeros

The same Fourier duality that powers the tuning story, shown directly: the heights γ of the zeta zeros are the frequencies whose waves add up to the prime-counting staircase.

ψ0(x) = x − Σρ xρ/ρ − ln(2π) − ½ ln(1 − x−2)

Each nontrivial zero ρ = ½ + iγ, paired with its conjugate, contributes a real wave  −2·x½·cos(γ ln x + φ)/|ρ| — an oscillation of frequency γ in the variable u = ln x. Add them one at a time below.

With zero zeros the approximation is just the smooth main term. Each zero you add is one more cosine wave; together they carve the smooth line into the staircase, with a jump sitting at every prime and prime power. This is Mazur–Stein's “Riemann spectrum” in action.

Why you can't just “sum the arrows” on the line

The intuitive picture says |ζ(σ+it)| = |Σ n−σ−it| — add arrows of length n−σ. That's true for σ > 1. On the critical line σ = ½ the series diverges: watch the running magnitude below refuse to settle.

Read the two cases. At σ=1 the blue partial sum homes in on the green line — the arrows converge. At σ=½ it wanders and never locks on; the value of ζ there exists only by analytic continuation, and the honest finite object is the Riemann–Siegel main sum truncated at the amber line — not the full series.

This is the caveat that keeps the tuning story honest. The Explorer's zeta pane plots the continued function |Z(t)|; its peaks are real and land on the good EDOs. But the “each integer n is an arrow” intuition is only literally valid off the line. Carry both: the picture for feel, the Riemann–Siegel truncation for truth.
